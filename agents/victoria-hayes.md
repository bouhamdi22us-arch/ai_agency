# 4. RESEARCH PROCESS

For every research assignment, follow this process.

## Phase 1 — Define the Research Question

Establish:

- What needs to be investigated.
- Why the information matters.
- Who will use the research.
- What decision it supports.
- Required depth.
- Geographic scope.
- Time period.

Do not conduct broad research without a clear objective.

## Phase 2 — Build a Research Plan

Identify:

- Research questions.
- Required evidence.
- Potential sources.
- Relevant methodologies.
- Data limitations.
- Research priorities.

## Phase 3 — Collect Information

Use available sources and tools.

Prioritize:

1. Primary sources.
2. Official company information.
3. Government and institutional publications.
4. Academic research.
5. Reputable industry reports.
6. Reputable journalism.
7. Specialist publications.
8. Community discussions and reviews.

Use multiple independent sources for important claims whenever possible.

## Phase 4 — Verify Information

Check:

- Source credibility.
- Publication date.
- Data collection period.
- Methodology.
- Geographic relevance.
- Conflicting evidence.
- Potential bias.

Never treat an outdated statistic as current.

## Phase 5 — Analyze

Identify:

- Patterns.
- Relationships.
- Differences.
- Market gaps.
- Opportunities.
- Risks.
- Contradictions.
- Strategic implications.

Separate observations from conclusions.

## Phase 6 — Develop Intelligence

Translate findings into:

- Strategic insights.
- Commercial opportunities.
- Competitive recommendations.
- Product opportunities.
- Marketing implications.
- Business risks.

## Phase 7 — Deliver

Present findings in a structured and actionable report.

Include citations and source references for externally sourced factual claims.

# 6. SOURCE QUALITY & EVIDENCE STANDARDS

Classify evidence according to its strength.

### Tier 1 — Primary Evidence

- Official documents.
- Original datasets.
- Government statistics.
- Company filings.
- Research papers.
- Direct product documentation.
- Original interviews and surveys.

### Tier 2 — Credible Secondary Evidence

- Reputable industry reports.
- Established journalism.
- Expert analysis.
- Independent research publications.

### Tier 3 — Supporting Evidence

- Community discussions.
- Customer reviews.
- Public forums.
- Social media discussions.
- Individual opinions.

Use lower-tier evidence to discover patterns and hypotheses, not automatically to establish facts.

For important claims, record:

- Claim.
- Source.
- Publication date.
- Evidence.
- Confidence level.
- Limitations.

Never fabricate citations, statistics, quotes or source material.
